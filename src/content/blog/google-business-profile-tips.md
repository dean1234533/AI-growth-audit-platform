Most local SEO advice starts and ends with "claim your Google Business Profile" — true, but not nearly enough. Here's what actually moves the needle once your profile is claimed.

## 1. Complete every single field

An incomplete profile is a weaker signal than a complete one. Business category (choose the most specific one available, plus relevant secondary categories), hours, service areas, attributes, and a genuine business description all contribute to how confidently Google matches your listing to relevant searches.

## 2. Keep your NAP consistent everywhere

Your Name, Address and Phone number (NAP) should match exactly — not just similarly — across your Google Business Profile, your website, and any directory listings. Inconsistencies (a suite number here, an abbreviated street name there) can quietly weaken how much Google trusts the listing.

## 3. Actively collect reviews — don't just wait for them

Review count and recency both matter. Businesses that ask satisfied customers directly (a simple follow-up text or email with a direct review link) consistently out-collect those that passively hope reviews accumulate. Respond to every review, positive or negative — it signals an active, engaged business.

## 4. Add photos regularly

Listings with more, more recent photos get more engagement in search results. Genuine photos of your team, premises and completed work outperform generic stock imagery, and Google explicitly favours profiles that stay active over ones that were set up once and never touched again.

## 5. Use Google Posts for updates and offers

Google Business Profile lets you publish short updates directly to your listing — offers, news, events. It's underused by most small businesses, which makes it a relatively easy way to stand out and show ongoing activity.

## 6. Match your website to your profile

Your Google Business Profile and your website should reinforce each other — the same NAP details, ideally an embedded map, and for multi-area businesses, website content specific to each area your profile lists as a service area. Our [free local SEO checker](/) checks exactly this alignment.

## 7. Monitor and respond to questions

The Q&A section on your profile is public and anyone can post — including, sometimes, competitors or people asking questions with no answer visible. Checking and answering these regularly prevents unanswered (or wrong) answers from sitting there indefinitely.

## The bottom line

A Google Business Profile isn't a "set once and forget" listing — the businesses that win local search treat it as an active, ongoing part of their marketing, not a one-time checkbox.
